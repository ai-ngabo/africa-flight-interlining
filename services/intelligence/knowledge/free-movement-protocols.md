# African free-movement protocols (summary for retrieval)

> Seed knowledge written for the MVP. Not legal advice. Verify against official sources.

## AU Protocol on Free Movement of Persons (2018)
The African Union adopted the Protocol to the Treaty Establishing the African Economic Community
relating to Free Movement of Persons, Right of Residence and Right of Establishment in January 2018
(Addis Ababa). It needs 15 ratifications to enter into force and has had only a handful, so it is
**not in force**. Travellers cannot rely on it at a border. The African Passport also exists only
in limited diplomatic use.

## ECOWAS Protocol on Free Movement (1979)
Citizens of ECOWAS member states may enter any other member state without a visa for up to
**90 days** with a valid passport or ECOWAS national ID / ECOWAS biometric ID card.
Members: Benin, Cabo Verde, Côte d'Ivoire, The Gambia, Ghana, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Liberia,
Nigeria, Senegal, Sierra Leone, Togo. Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger withdrew in January 2025 and
formed the Alliance of Sahel States (AES); transitional arrangements on free movement have been
announced but should be verified before travel.

Typical land corridor: Lagos – Cotonou – Lomé – Accra – Abidjan (Abidjan–Lagos corridor), served by
coach operators. Expect checks at Seme (NG/BJ), Hillacondji (BJ/TG), Aflao (TG/GH) and Elubo (GH/CI).

## EAC Common Market Protocol (2010)
East African Community citizens can travel between partner states visa-free, in several cases
using a national ID card instead of a passport (Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda). Members: Kenya, Uganda,
Rwanda, Tanzania, Burundi, South Sudan, DR Congo (joined 2022), Somalia (joined 2024) — free movement
implementation for the newest members is partial.

### East Africa Tourist Visa
Non-EAC visitors can buy a single **East Africa Tourist Visa** covering Kenya, Uganda and Rwanda,
valid 90 days, multiple entry among the three countries. It must be first used in the country that
issued it.

## CEMAC
Citizens of Cameroon, Gabon, Republic of Congo, Chad, Central African Republic and Equatorial
Guinea have visa-free movement within CEMAC (implemented 2017).

## SADC
SADC has a Protocol on the Facilitation of Movement of Persons (2005) but no single visa. Most
movement is governed by bilateral visa waivers, so many — but not all — SADC nationals travel
visa-free within the region. South Africa exempts most SADC nationals for 30–90 days.

### KAZA UNIVISA
A single visa covering **Zambia and Zimbabwe** (KAZA UNIVISA), valid 30 days, including day trips to
Botswana via the Kazungula border. Available to eligible nationalities on arrival at major entry
points.

## Unilateral visa openness
Several states have unilaterally opened to all Africans, notably Rwanda, Benin, The Gambia,
Seychelles (travel authorisation still required), and announcements by Ghana and Kenya. The African
Development Bank's Africa Visa Openness Index tracks progress yearly.
