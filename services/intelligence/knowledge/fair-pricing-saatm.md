# Air fares in Africa, SAATM and fair-pricing transparency

> Seed knowledge for the MVP. Figures are directional; cite current studies before publishing.

## Why intra-African fares are high
- **Limited liberalisation.** The Yamoussoukro Decision (1999) aimed to liberalise intra-African air
  services. The Single African Air Transport Market (SAATM, launched 2018) operationalises it, and
  over 35 states have signed up, but many bilateral air service agreements still restrict capacity
  and fifth-freedom rights.
- **Taxes and charges.** Passenger charges, security and departure taxes in several African airports
  are among the highest in the world relative to fare levels (studies by IATA and AFRAA highlight
  this).
- **Routing via distant hubs.** Lack of direct links forces long detours (sometimes via Europe or the
  Middle East), raising price and travel time.
- **Low competition per route** and high fuel/handling costs.

## What the platform does
- Shows a **transparent fare breakdown**: carrier base fare, airport taxes/charges, platform fee
  (capped at 3% of base fare, maximum USD 12) and optional protection premium.
- Computes a **Fair Price Index**: each leg's fare is compared with the median fare-per-kilometre of
  comparable-distance legs in the network. Legs more than 35% above the benchmark are flagged.
- Finds **cheaper multi-carrier and multi-modal combinations** (e.g. flight + coach across the ECOWAS
  corridor) that a single airline will never show.
- Settles in **local currencies via PAPSS** to avoid double conversion through USD.
